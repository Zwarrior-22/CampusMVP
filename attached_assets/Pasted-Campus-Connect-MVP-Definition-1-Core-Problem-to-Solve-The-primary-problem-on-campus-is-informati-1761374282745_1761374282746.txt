Campus Connect: MVP Definition

1. Core Problem to Solve

The primary problem on campus is information chaos. Students miss important notices, clubs struggle to promote events, and there is no single, trusted source for "what's happening."

The MVP will solve this by creating a single, filterable, real-time source of truth for campus announcements and events.

2. Target Users (Initial Focus)

Students (as Consumers): The primary audience. They need a simple, centralized way to see what's going on.

Club Organizers (as Creators): A key group that needs an audience. They are the early adopters who will provide content.

Faculty/Admin (as Creators): The source of official, "must-see" information (e.g., fee deadlines, exam schedules).

3. MVP Features

Feature 1: The Unified Hub (The "What's On" Board)

This feature directly combines the "Centralized Event & Notice Board" and the "Club Space" (but only the announcement part).

For Students (The Feed):

A single, mobile-friendly webpage.

A real-time feed of all posts, with the newest at the top.

Simple toggle filters: All, Official Notices, Club Events.

A search bar.

For Creators (Clubs & Admin):

A simple, secure login.

A "Create Post" button that opens a simple form.

Post type selector:

Notice: Just a title and a body of text.

Event: Title, body, plus Date, Time, and Location fields.

Events automatically appear in the feed and are highlighted on their day.

Feature 2: The "Suggestion Box" (Simple Feedback)

This is the MVP version of the "Smart Complaint System" and "Feedback System." Before building a complex system with routing, tickets, and statuses, we must first validate if students will use it and what they will submit.

For Students:

A single "Feedback / Suggestion" link in the navigation.

A simple, anonymous (or optional-name) form with:

A dropdown menu for Category (e.g., "Hostel," "Academics," "Campus," "Suggestion").

A message box.

On the Backend (MVP Logic):

The form doesn't create a ticket. It simply formats and sends an email to a single, designated admin account (e.g., dean.studentwelfare@college.edu).

This delivers immediate value (feedback is collected) with almost zero development complexity.

4. What is NOT in the MVP (Deferred for Phase 2)

Smart Complaint/Request System:

Why: The "Suggestion Box" (Feature 2) is the MVP. We will first collect data on the types and volume of complaints. If it gets heavy usage, we will build a full-fledged ticketing system in Phase 2.

Attendance or Timetable Tracker:

Why: This is a major technical project, not a communication one. It requires deep, secure integration with the college's private academic database (SIS). It's a "Future Vision" feature, not an MVP feature.

Club/Project Collaboration Space:

Why: The MVP (Feature 1) gives clubs a voice. It does not give them internal management tools (like chat, task lists, or file sharing). This is a "product within a product" and should be built later, after the central hub is successful.

5. Success Metrics (How we know it's working)

Adoption: 50% of students have visited the site within the first month.

Engagement (Creators): At least 10 new events or notices are posted per week by clubs and admin.

Validation (Feedback): At least 20 meaningful submissions are made through the "Suggestion Box" in the first month (this validates the need for Phase 2).
